# Halka customer discovery interview guide

**Purpose:** Understand how informal rotating savings circles are organized today, what problems
occur, and what people already do to solve them. This is not a product pitch or sales interview.

**Length:** 25–35 minutes. Discuss current behavior before showing Halka. Use the same core guide
for all five participants; use the individual follow-up tracks in `five-interview-packets.md` only
when they match the participant's actual experience.

## Opening and consent

> Thank you for taking the time. Halka is exploring how a testnet app might support rotating
> contribution groups such as altın günü. First, I want to understand how you handle this today;
> there are no right or wrong answers. You can skip any question or stop at any time. I will not
> ask for real-money details or personal financial information.
>
> We will use an anonymous participant code in our notes. Recording is optional and requires
> separate permission; declining a recording will not affect your participation. Is it okay to
> take notes? Separately, do you agree to a recording?

Record permission to take notes and permission to record separately. Do not record without clear
consent. Accept a refusal without discussion.

## Part A — Current behavior (before showing the prototype)

1. Tell me about the most recent altın günü or similar savings circle you took part in. How many
   people were involved, and how did the rotation work?
2. How was the group organized most recently? How were members, contribution amounts, and payout
   order communicated?
3. How did you track whether a contribution had been made? Tell me about the most recent time you
   needed to check.
4. Have there been misunderstandings, delays, or disagreements about contributions or payout
   order? Tell me about the latest example and how it was resolved.
5. Who holds or transfers the group contributions? What works well and what is difficult about
   that arrangement?
6. What happens when a member pays late or leaves the group? What happened in the most recent
   example?
7. Is there anything you would change about the current way of organizing the group? Why?

## Part B — Needs and perceived risk

8. What information would you need to see immediately before contributing? Where do you get that
   information today?
9. What might feel unsafe or confusing in a new digital tool? Can you recall a recent example of
   trying a similar tool?
10. Have you used Stellar, a crypto wallet, or transaction signing before? If yes, tell me about
    the most recent task. If not, what might stop you from trying it?

Do not end with hypothetical purchase questions such as “Would you use this?” If participants ask
about Halka, answer their question and mark that discussion separately in the notes.

## Part C — Halka prototype (only after Parts A and B)

Introduce the prototype neutrally: “This prototype runs on testnet and demonstrates the current
contract rules. It does not use real money.” Do not lead with a feature or benefit list.

11. Looking at this screen, what do you think is happening? What information led you to that
    conclusion?
12. What do you think will happen next? Under what condition do you think a payout can happen?
13. If you were about to connect a wallet or sign an action, what would you want to check first?
14. Who do you think controls the wallet keys in this flow? What on the screen led you to that
    belief?

Capture the participant's first interpretation, the text or controls they used, their questions,
and where they needed help. If you explain anything, let them finish their interpretation first
and record the intervention in the summary.

## Closing

> Is there anything important about organizing this kind of group that I have not asked about?
> Would you like to be contacted about a short testnet-only usability session later?

Do not put contact details in this repository or in anonymized summaries. Keep any necessary
follow-up contact information separately and securely; record only the participant code and
whether they agreed to follow-up here.

## After the interview

- Create one copy of `summary-template.md` and use only an anonymous code such as `I-01`–`I-05`.
- Separate direct observations or participant words from interpretation.
- Mark H1–H4 as a **signal** (supports / challenges / unclear); do not call one interview
  validation.
- Never store a participant's name, phone, email, wallet address, or secret key in the tracker.
